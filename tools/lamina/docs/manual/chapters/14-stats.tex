\chapter{Statistics}\label{ch:stats}
\begin{goals}
\textbf{In this chapter} you summarise one column exactly, every column at once, and the cells you have
selected, and learn which figures are exact and which are approximate.
\end{goals}

\section{One column}\label{sec:one-column}

Double-click the distribution above a column, or choose \ui{Statistics — …} on its header's or a cell's
menu. lamina scans the column (with a filter on, only the matching rows; the window's title then says
\ui{(filtered)}) and opens its statistics. A large file shows the scan's progress and a \ui{cancel}
button; \key{Esc} cancels too.

\shot[0.62\linewidth]{stats-cu}{The statistics of \col{Cu\_pct} on the sample: the histogram with its
quartiles drawn over it, and the figures below.}

\subsection{A number column}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lL@{}}
\toprule
\sffamily\bfseries figure & \sffamily\bfseries what it is \\
\midrule
count & rows scanned \\
non-null & rows with a number in this column \\
nulls & rows where it is blank \\
non-numeric & rows where it holds something that isn't a number, with up to twelve of them as examples
(\code{<0.01}, \code{n/a}, \code{-}); shown only when there are some \\

min, max, mean, sum & over the numbers \\
std & the sample standard deviation, $n-1$ in the denominator \\
CV & the coefficient of variation, std divided by the absolute mean \\

p5, p25, median, p75, p95 & the quantiles \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Above the figures is the column's histogram, 40 bars, with the middle half of the values (p25 to p75)
shaded and the median marked. \ui{log x} draws it on a log scale of value, for a skewed grade.
\ui{copy} copies the figures as tab-separated text; \ui{png} saves the histogram as an image.

\subsection{Leaving out zeros and negatives}

A grade column often holds zeros for unestimated or waste blocks and negative codes for missing values,
and both distort its statistics. Tick \ui{exclude zeros}, \ui{exclude negatives} or both, and click
\ui{apply}: the column is scanned again without them. They are counted under \ui{excluded}, not hidden.

\subsection{A text column}

For text, the window gives the count, the blanks and the number of distinct values (\ui{+} after it when
there are more than 100{,}000 and lamina stopped counting), and the most common values with their counts
and shares. Click values in that list to build a filter of them, one click to add a value and another to
take it away: \code{LITO in "OXIDE", "TRANSITION"}. The grid follows as you click.

\subsection{How the figures are computed}\label{sec:stats-maths}

The mean, standard deviation and skewness are computed in one pass with Welford's method
\parencite{welford1962}: running sums of the deviations from the running mean, which stay accurate where
summing squares would lose digits (a column of UTM northings, say). For $n$ values with mean
$\bar{x}$,
\[
s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}}, \qquad
g_1 = \frac{\sqrt{n}\,\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^3}{\left(\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2\right)^{3/2}} .
\]
The skewness $g_1$ is the moment form, without the small-sample correction some packages apply; on more
than a few hundred values the two agree.

The quantiles are exact: lamina keeps the column's numbers as it scans, sorts them, and takes the
\term{nearest rank}, the value at position $\mathrm{round}(p(n-1))$ counting from 0. It doesn't
interpolate between neighbours, so on a short column the median can differ a little from a package that
does. Keeping the numbers is what costs memory here: eight bytes a value. Past 5{,}242{,}880 values lamina
stops keeping them, and the window says \ui{quantiles: too many values}; every other figure is still
exact. Filter first to get the quantiles of a subset.

\section{Every column: the column summary}\label{sec:summary}

\ui{Σ stats} on the toolbar, or \menu{Tools>Column summary (Σ stats)…}, scans the whole table once for
every column at the same time, and opens the \term{column summary}: one row per column.

\shot{summary}{The column summary of the sample: one row per column, sortable by any figure.}

Its columns are \ui{type} (\ui{num} or \ui{txt}), \ui{n}, \ui{null\%}, \ui{non-num}, \ui{min}, \ui{max},
\ui{mean}, \ui{std}, \ui{CV}, \ui{skew}, \ui{zero\%}, \ui{p50}, and for text, \ui{distinct}. Click a column
heading to sort by it: by \ui{null\%} or \ui{non-num} to find the gaps and the junk in a file you have just
received, by \ui{CV} to find the erratic grades. Click a row for that column's own statistics, or its
name to scroll the grid to it. \ui{copy all} copies the table.

The summary makes two passes, not one per column: the first for counts, sums and the range of each
column, the second for a histogram of each between the range the first found. So it costs two reads of
the file however many columns there are, and its figures fill a cache: afterwards, any column's
statistics open at once. \ui{Σ stats}' \ui{▾} offers \ui{Precompute only (warm cache)}: the two passes
without opening the summary, so that later statistics are instant.

\subsection{What is approximate}

Everything in the summary is exact except the median. Keeping every value of every column would cost
too much memory, so the summary's \ui{p50} is read off the column's histogram, interpolating inside the
bar that holds the middle value, and is marked \ui{≈}. A column's statistics opened from the cache show
the same \ui{≈} quantiles, with \ui{compute exact} to scan that column properly.

\begin{caution}
On a skewed grade the \ui{≈} median can be far off. The histogram has 40 equal bars between the
minimum and the maximum, and when most values sit in the first bar, the interpolation inside it says
little: on the sample, \col{Cu\_pct}'s summary median is 0.134 where the exact median is 0.042. Treat
\ui{p50 ≈} as a rough figure, and open the column's statistics (\ui{compute exact}) before quoting it.
\end{caution}

\section{The selection}\label{sec:selection-stats}

The quickest summary is the selection line (\cref{sec:selection}): select cells, and the status bar gives
their count, sum, mean, minimum and maximum, over the first 50{,}000 cells of a large selection.

\begin{tryit}
Meet a file in a minute.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Open the sample and click \ui{Σ stats}.
\item Sort the summary by \ui{CV}, highest first: the three grades \col{Ag\_gpt}, \col{Au\_gpt} and
\col{Cu\_pct} lead, then \col{ore\_pct}; \col{density}, the coordinates and the constant block sizes
trail.
\item Click the \col{Au\_gpt} row. Its statistics open from the cache, with \ui{≈} quantiles. Click
\ui{compute exact}.
\item Tick \ui{exclude zeros} and \ui{apply}: the quarter of the blocks with no gold leave the mean, and
the median rises.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tryit}
