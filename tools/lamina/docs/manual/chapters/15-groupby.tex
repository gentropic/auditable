\chapter{Group by}\label{ch:groupby}
\begin{goals}
\textbf{In this chapter} you summarise value columns per group (per lithology, per domain, per bench),
with a weighted mean beside the plain one.
\end{goals}

\menu{Tools>Group by…} answers ``what does each group look like?'': for every distinct value of one
column, statistics of the others.

\section{Setting it up}\label{sec:groupby-setup}

\begin{description}
\item[\ui{Group by}] the column whose values make the groups. Usually a category (\col{LITO},
\col{ZONE}, a domain code), but any column will do: a number column gives one group per distinct number,
which is what a banded calculated column is for (\cref{sec:bands}).
\item[\ui{Value}] a number column to summarise. \ui{+ add variable} adds another; \ui{✕} removes one.
\item[\ui{Weight by}] optionally, a number column to weight the values by: the sample length for
assays, the tonnes for blocks.
\end{description}

Click \ui{Group}. lamina scans the view once (with a filter on, only its rows; the title says
\ui{(filtered)}) and fills the table.

\shot{groupby}{Group by \col{LITO} on the sample, values \col{Cu\_pct} and \col{Au\_gpt}, weighted by
\col{density}: each value's sum, mean, weighted mean, standard deviation, minimum and maximum, per
lithology, and the total.}

\section{Reading the table}\label{sec:groupby-table}

One row per group and a \ui{Σ total} row for all of them. For each value column: \ui{sum}, \ui{mean},
\ui{std} (the sample standard deviation), \ui{min} and \ui{max}, and with a weight, \ui{wmean}, the
weighted mean
\[
\bar{x}_w = \frac{\sum w_i x_i}{\sum w_i}
\]
over the rows with both a value and a weight. \ui{n} counts the group's rows.

Click a column heading to sort the groups by it; click again to reverse. \ui{copy all} copies the table
as tab-separated text.

\begin{description}
\item[Blanks] in the group column make a group of their own, \ui{(blank)}. A blank value is left out of
that value's figures; a row with a blank weight is left out of the weighted mean but stays in the plain
one.
\item[Text in a value column] is left out of its figures, as a blank is.
\end{description}

\begin{why}
The plain and the weighted mean sit side by side because the difference between them is the finding.
Assays of unequal length averaged without weights give each sample the same say whatever its length; if
the short samples are the rich ones (as at a contact, where they often are), the plain mean overstates the
grade. When \ui{mean} and \ui{wmean} disagree, that is worth knowing before anything is estimated.
\end{why}

\section{From a group to its rows}

Click a group's row: the window closes and the grid is filtered to that group (\code{LITO == "OXIDE"}, or
\code{LITO is blank} for the blank group).

\begin{caution}
That filter replaces the current one. If you grouped a filtered view, clicking a group shows the whole
group, not its part of the filtered view: add the earlier condition back in the filter box.
\end{caution}

\section{Limits}

lamina keeps up to 1{,}000 groups. A column with more distinct values (a block identifier, a raw grade)
stops adding groups at the thousandth, its later new values are counted only in the total, and the table
says it was truncated. Band such a column first (\cref{sec:bands}).

\begin{tryit}
Weighted and plain, by lithology.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Open the sample, then \menu{Tools>Group by…}. \ui{Group by} is \col{LITO}.
\item Set \ui{Value} to \col{Cu\_pct}, add \col{Au\_gpt}, and set \ui{Weight by} to \col{density}. Click
\ui{Group}.
\item Compare \ui{Cu\_pct·mean} and \ui{Cu\_pct·wmean} for TRANSITION: about 0.475 against 0.487. The
denser blocks are a little richer.
\item Sort by \ui{Cu\_pct·sum}, then click the SULPHIDE row to see its blocks.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tryit}
