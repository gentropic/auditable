\chapter{Grade–tonnage}\label{ch:gt}
\begin{goals}
\textbf{In this chapter} you report a block model's tonnes, grade and contained metal by domain, and its
grade–tonnage curve, straight from the file, and learn exactly what is summed.
\end{goals}

\menu{Tools>Grade–tonnage…} is the resource view of a block model: how many tonnes there are, at what
grade, holding how much metal, by domain and above each cutoff. It reads the model as it is, with no
import and no conversion, and respects the filter, so it reports a pit shell, a bench or a domain as
readily as the whole model.

\section{What a block weighs}\label{sec:gt-weight}

The report needs one number per block: its tonnes. lamina works it out as
\[
t_i = V_i \times \rho_i \times p_i ,
\]
the block's volume, its density and its ore proportion. Each is an expression (\cref{ch:expr}), so each can
be a column (\code{density}), a number (\code{2.7}), or a formula (\code{dx * dy * dz},
\code{interp(Fe\_pct, 20, 2.7, 65, 4.3)}). A factor left blank counts as 1.

When the window opens, lamina fills them in from the column names it recognises:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lL@{}}
\toprule
\sffamily\bfseries factor & \sffamily\bfseries filled in with \\
\midrule
\ui{Volume} & \code{dx * dy * dz} when there are columns named \col{dx}, \col{dy} and \col{dz}; else a
column whose name contains \code{vol}; else 1 \\
\ui{Density} & a column whose name contains \code{dens}, or is \col{SG}; else 2.7 \\
\ui{Ore proportion} & a column named like \col{ore}, \col{prop} or \col{ore\_pct}; else 1 \\
\ui{Group by} & the first text column; or \ui{— whole deposit —} \\
\ui{Grade} & the first number column that isn't an identifier, a coordinate or a block size \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Check them: a guess from a name is only a guess.

\subsection{Density units}\label{sec:gt-density-unit}

Beside \ui{Density} is its unit: \ui{t/m³ (=g/cm³)}, the default, \ui{kg/m³} or \ui{lb/ft³}. lamina converts
to t/m³ before multiplying, so a density column in kg/m³ (2650 rather than 2.65) doesn't make the model a
thousand times heavier. The formula line under the report shows the conversion when there is one:
\code{[kg/m³ → t/m³]}.

\subsection{Grades and their units}\label{sec:gt-grade-unit}

Each \ui{Grade} has a unit: \ui{\%}, \ui{g/t}, \ui{ppm}, \ui{oz/t} (troy ounces per short ton), or none.
\ui{+ add grade} adds another grade to report; \ui{✕} removes one. With a unit, the report labels the
grade and gives contained metal in tonnes. Without one, metal is reported as tonnes times grade, in
whatever the grade's unit is, and the column is headed \ui{Au\_gpt·t}.

\section{The report}\label{sec:gt-report}

Click \ui{Report}. One pass over the view gives, per group and in total:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lL@{}}
\toprule
\sffamily\bfseries column & \sffamily\bfseries is \\
\midrule
\ui{blocks} & the blocks with tonnes greater than zero \\
\ui{tonnes} & $T = \sum t_i$ \\
the grade & the tonnage-weighted mean, $\bar{g} = \sum t_i g_i \,/\, \sum t_i$ over the blocks that have a
grade \\
metal & $M = \sum t_i\,g_i$, times the unit's fraction when the grade has a unit (0.01 for \%,
$10^{-6}$ for g/t and ppm) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\shot{gt-report}{Grade–tonnage on the sample, by lithology: tonnes, the tonnage-weighted grade and the
metal per domain, and below it the cutoff curve and table for \col{Au\_gpt}.}

Click a column heading to sort the groups by it. \ui{copy all} copies the report.

\subsection{What is left out, and why it matters}\label{sec:gt-left-out}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Blocks with no tonnes} ($t_i \le 0$, or blank because a factor is blank) are left out
entirely: they have no mass. On the sample, the 2{,}391 waste blocks with an ore proportion of 0 are not
in the WASTE row, which counts 55{,}400 blocks where group by counts 57{,}791.
\item \textbf{Blocks with tonnes but no grade} count in \ui{tonnes} and not in the grade's mean or metal.
So when some grades are blank, metal is $\bar{g}$ times the tonnes \emph{with} a grade, which is less
than $\bar{g} \times T$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{caution}
That second rule is deliberate (a missing grade is not a zero grade, \cref{sec:blanks}), but it means a
report over blocks with blank grades has two tonnages in it: the one in the \ui{tonnes} column, and the
one the grade and metal refer to. If the blank grades are unestimated blocks that should be out of the
report, filter them out first: \code{Au\_gpt is filled}.
\end{caution}

\section{The cutoff curve}\label{sec:gt-curve}

Below the report, each grade gets its grade–tonnage curve over the whole view (not per group): the
tonnes at or above each cutoff, falling as the cutoff rises, and the mean grade of those tonnes, rising.
Under it, a table at round cutoffs:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lL@{}}
\toprule
\sffamily\bfseries column & \sffamily\bfseries is, for cutoff $c$ \\
\midrule
\ui{tonnes ≥} & $T(c) = \sum_{g_i \ge c} t_i$ \\
the grade & $\bar{g}(c) = \sum_{g_i \ge c} t_i g_i \,/\, T(c)$ \\
metal & $\bar{g}(c)\,T(c)$, in the grade's unit as above \\
\ui{\% of tonnes} & $T(c)$ as a share of $T(0)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The curve is computed in a second pass, with the block grades put in bins of a round width (1, 2, 2.5 or
5 times a power of ten, chosen to give at most about 150 bins) from zero (or from the lowest grade, if some are negative) to
the highest. The table's automatic cutoffs are bin edges, about eight of them, so its figures are exact,
not read off the curve; rows past the last tonnes are dropped.

\subsection{Your own cutoffs}\label{sec:gt-cutoffs}

Type cutoffs in the box beside a grade, separated by commas, \code{0.3, 0.5, 1, 2}, and click
\ui{Report}. The table then gives exactly those cutoffs, each accumulated exactly, every block with a grade
at or above it, not snapped to a bin. Leave the box empty for the automatic ones.

\subsection{Fixing the plot's ranges}\label{sec:gt-ranges}

The box after the cutoffs fixes the plot's axes, so this month's curve can be laid over last month's at
the same scale. It takes up to three settings, separated by spaces:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\ttfamily}lL@{}}
\toprule
\normalfont\sffamily\bfseries write & \sffamily\bfseries fixes \\
\midrule
x:0..2 & the cutoff axis, from 0 to 2 \\
t:5e6 & the tonnes axis, up to 5 million (\code{t:1e6..5e6} for both ends) \\
g:3 & the grade axis, up to 3 (\code{g:1..3} for both ends) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

An empty box, or a setting left out, is automatic.

\subsection{Taking it away}

Above each curve, \ui{copy} copies its table as tab-separated text, \ui{copy png} puts the chart on the
clipboard as a picture, and \ui{save png} saves it as an image file. Neither touches the network: the
picture is drawn in the page.

\section{Grade–tonnage in a lens}

A lens keeps the grade–tonnage setup: the factors, their units, the group, and each grade with its unit,
cutoffs and ranges (\cref{ch:lens}). Applied to next month's model, it opens grade–tonnage set up as it
was, and \ui{Report} gives the same report on the new numbers.

\begin{tryit}
The sample's resource above 0.5~g/t gold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Open the sample and \menu{Tools>Grade–tonnage…}. The factors read \code{dx * dy * dz},
\code{density} and \code{ore\_pct}; the group \col{LITO}; the grade \col{Au\_gpt}. Set its unit to
\ui{g/t}.
\item Type \code{0.5, 1, 2} as the cutoffs and click \ui{Report}. The total is about 20.3 million tonnes,
by lithology above, with the curve and the three cutoffs below.
\item Add a grade, \col{Cu\_pct}, unit \ui{\%}, and report again: copper metal in tonnes beside the gold.
\item Close the window, filter \code{Z >= 500}, and report again: the upper half of the model only.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tryit}
