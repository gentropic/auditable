\chapter{Data quality}\label{ch:dq}
\begin{goals}
\textbf{In this chapter} you check a file for the quiet faults that spoil an estimate without anyone
noticing: codes that lost their leading zeros, junk in numeric columns, missing-value codes, and more.
\end{goals}

\menu{Tools>Data quality…} reads a sample of the file (or, with a filter on, every filtered row) and flags
each column that shows a known kind of fault. Every flag is something that has, somewhere, ruined an
estimate quietly: a hole called \code{007} that became \code{7} and stopped joining to its collar, a
$-999$ averaged in as a grade.

missing-value code.}

\section{What it checks}\label{sec:dq-checks}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}llL@{}}
\toprule
\sffamily\bfseries flag & \sffamily\bfseries level & \sffamily\bfseries the rule \\
\midrule
wrong number of fields & high & rows with more or fewer fields than the table has columns: a stray
delimiter, a broken quote, a short line. Reported once, for \ui{(every column)}, with how many rows and
the first of them \\
all blank & high & every value in the sample is empty \\
leading zeros lost & high & a number column has values written with a leading zero (\code{007},
\code{012}): codes, read as numbers \\
non-numeric values & warn & a number column has values that aren't numbers, with examples \\
thousands separators & warn & values like \code{1,234} in a number column, which with a decimal point
set read as text \\
whitespace padding & warn & values with spaces before or after them, which break filters and joins
(\code{"OXIDE "} isn't \code{"OXIDE"}) \\
possible sentinel & warn & the column's minimum is $-9$, $-99$, $-999$, $-9999$ or $-99999$ and occurs at
least twice: a missing-value code, not a measurement \\
constant & info & every value is the same \\
dates stored as text & info & at least 80\% of a text column's values look like dates
(\code{2024-03-15}, \code{2024/3/15}); lamina has no date type, so they sort and compare as text \\
high blank rate & info & half the column or more is blank \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The flags are listed high first, then warnings, then information, in column order within each. A column
with nothing wrong isn't listed; a file with nothing wrong says \ui{✓}.

\section{The sample}\label{sec:dq-sample}

Without a filter, the check reads the same spread sample of 8{,}192 rows the distributions are drawn from
(\cref{sec:gutter-sample}): quick on any file, and spread through it, but a sample. A fault in a few rows
can be missed. With a filter on, it reads every row the filter keeps, so to check a whole large file
exactly, filter it to everything a column can hold (\code{ID is filled}) first; that is a full scan.

\section{Acting on a flag}\label{sec:dq-act}

Each flag's \ui{first at} column names the first row the fault was seen in, with two links: \ui{go} to
that row in the grid, and \ui{raw} to its bytes in the raw view (\cref{ch:raw}), where a stray delimiter
or a broken quote can be seen as it is. Click a flag's row for the column's statistics, which show the
non-numeric values and the distribution (\cref{sec:one-column}); click the column's name to scroll the
grid to it. \ui{copy all} copies the list, and \ui{⧉ pin} keeps it as a tab (\cref{sec:pins}).

\ui{leading zeros lost} carries \ui{fix: treat as text}: one click, and the column is read as text, its
codes back as they were written (\cref{sec:text-number}). The others have no button, because the fix
depends on what the value means:

\begin{description}
\item[A sentinel] leave it out where it matters, \code{nullif(VAL, -999)} as a calculated column, or a
filter \code{VAL != -999}; or exclude negatives in its statistics.
\item[Thousands separators] check the file's decimal setting (\cref{sec:decimal-comma}): a file written
with a decimal comma shows its numbers this way when read with a point.
\item[Whitespace] \code{trim(LITO)} as a calculated column, and filter or group by that.
\item[Non-numeric values] see them in the statistics; decide whether \code{<0.01} means half the
detection limit, zero, or a blank, and make a calculated column that says so.
\end{description}

\begin{tryit}
A file with two faults.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Save these lines as \file{codes.csv}:
\begin{codeblock}
CODE,VAL,FLAG
007,1.5,a
012,-999,b
013,-999,c
100,2.5,d
\end{codeblock}
\item Open it in lamina. \col{CODE} shows 7, 12, 13, 100.
\item Open \menu{Tools>Data quality…}. Two flags: \col{CODE}, leading zeros lost, three values; \col{VAL},
a possible sentinel, $-999$ twice.
\item Click \ui{fix: treat as text} on the first. \col{CODE} reads \code{007}, \code{012}, \code{013},
\code{100}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tryit}
